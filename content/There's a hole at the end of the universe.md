There's a hole at the end of the universe. What do I mean by this?

When you keep on searching deeply about something, you'll reach the end where you find a rule or something that you have to accept as being fundamentally true for the thing to make sense in the bigger picture.

Let's talk about a rock. At a normal scale, it's a rock. You want to learn deeper about the rock. So you cut it into pieces. You want to know what the pieces are made up of, so you crush it into dirt. It seems dirt makes pieces, pieces make rock. You want to know what dirt is made up of, you put it into a microscope, you see small particles, what are the particles made of, you research and find they're made of electrons, protons and neutrons, well what are they made of, they are made of charges, idk, maybe something else. Then there is something even more fundamental. At the end, I think right now we think that the fundamental truth (according to string theory, and bear with me cause I am no physicist) is that there is a pattern of certain energy waves. The electrons are made up of that, atoms of electrons, dirt of atoms, pieces of dirt and finally rocks are made of pieces.

At the end of the universe (or the search for the meaning of life), there's a hole that can't be explained and has to be taken as the fundamental truth. The hole is just there which when looked at a microscopic view seems meaningless, but on the bigger scale, it gives rise to the universe somehow.

I want to connect this concept to [[Bhagwat Gita]], bible, scriptures, quoran or any other religious book that tells us to take it as the fundamental truth and not to ask but believe them.

I've recently been re-interested in [[Bhagwat Gita]], or maybe hinduism. Like how does hinduism define life. What is this deep dissatisfaction that I'm feeling. Why do I want to pursue wealth but the pursuit seems so meaningless. With ChatGPT simplifying the texts, I now know that Krishna says something along this line to Arjun, "You have no control over the result, but action is your duty. Also, don't get attached to inaction."

Maybe this is how hinduism defines a life that feels content and meaningful. He says these lines, maybe he does not necessarily expand on it because that's the hole that I'm talking about. For example, let's see the line "don't get attached to inaction".

[[You sit in a chair and grow old]]. There's no harm in doing that, but what's the point in just sitting in a chair? You can argue what's the point in doing the action as well, but maybe there is no point, but maybe doing action is what makes a human feel content with life. We'll just have to take Krishna's word for it, follow it (it's up to the person if they want to religiously follow it or just follow it as a guide to live a happy life without devoting their life to god and religion), and see if we actually feel content.
