I've been thinking about [[Bhagwat Gita]] and the story that plays out.

Arjun is in the war ground with his family in the opposite side. It's so meaningless to fight this war. Why can't he just get out of it if the war is so meaningless? He should just be able to simply talk to his brothers and make them understand that this is all so meaningless, that life has no inherent meaning. We can just try to be happy with what we are and try to love everyone. Like [[Osho]] says to just enjoy life, dance, sing and be happy.

But the position that Arjun is, is a position we find ourselves in meaningless places like when we have to work in an office. Why work when you can just not work and enjoy life.

Maybe this is the parallelism between Bhagwat Gita and meaninglessness of life.

Maybe this is why some lines of Gita is resonating with me.

From [Bhagavad-gītā As It Is 2.14 \| Vedabase](https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/14/):
> In the proper discharge of duty, one has to learn to tolerate nonpermanent appearances and disappearances of happiness and distress. According to Vedic injunction, one has to take his bath early in the morning even during the month of Māgha (January-February). It is very cold at that time, but in spite of that a man who abides by the religious principles does not hesitate to take his bath. Similarly, a woman does not hesitate to cook in the kitchen in the months of May and June, the hottest part of the summer season. One has to execute his duty in spite of climatic inconveniences.

Maybe it isn't that we one HAS to do something. Maybe it is just that doing those things will help us live a life that we're happy about. That satisfied feeling, that content feeling, maybe it all arises from doing so. We don't HAVE to bathe in the morning in the winter, but if we take it as a duty and do it anyway, it leads to a life that is full of content. It affects other aspects of life like discipline and action without being attached to result. [[You don't have control over the results]].
