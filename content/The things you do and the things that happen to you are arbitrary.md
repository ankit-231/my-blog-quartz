What [[Alan Watts|Watts]] says is: "the things you do and the things that happen to you are arbitrary". He means to say about breath. You feel that you breathe, but if you notice, even when you're not consciously breathing, breathing just happens to you.

What I interpreted is also that we think, but thinking happens to us as well. Almost like catching a ball and dropping our hands after catching it in cricket means that ball was dropping by itself, then we caught it and we could now control the speed of the ball, but we let it go down a bit in its natural direction. The moment between the ball is caught and when we completely stop the ball is almost same as we catching ourselves thinking and trying to stop it, but becoming conscious that we are breathing and suddenly controlling our breath.

I think that Watts means to apply this to thinking. Thinking happens to us, and we can put our mind to something and think as well, like solving a maths equation.

It does feel scary now because it does not seem to be in my control, the thinking does not seem to be in my control. My mind just seems to drift into thoughts even when meditating. Watts says to just observe and not do anything else.
